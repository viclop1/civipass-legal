Civipass — Privacy policy

Last updated: 24 November 2026 · Version française (reference text)

In short

Civipass works without an account, without ads and without analytics tools. Your answers and your progress are stored on your phone; if your phone's backups are turned on, they may be included in them. The app sends no data to Civipass or to any provider: the full version is bought directly from the App Store or Google Play, from your phone. We only receive those stores' sales reports and the messages you write to us.

Who is responsible for your data

The controller is Víctor López Rodríguez, an independent developer based in Madrid (Spain). For any question about your data: civipassapp@gmail.com.

Data stored locally

The app stores locally, on your device:

  • your answers to the questions and your progress by topic;
  • the results of your mock exams;
  • your settings: the procedure you are preparing for (multi-year residence permit, resident card or naturalisation) and the language of the explanations;
  • if you bought the full version, the confirmation of that purchase, so the app also works offline;
  • if a purchase is awaiting confirmation (a parent's approval or a payment in progress, for instance), the date it started waiting, for seven days at most.

This information stays on your phone: the app does not send it to Civipass or to anyone, and we have no access to it. The procedure you are preparing for may say something about your immigration situation: one more reason why it never leaves your phone. Uninstalling the app, or clearing its data in your phone's settings, erases what it has stored on your phone, but not backups already made (see the next paragraph).

If you have turned on your phone's backups (iCloud on iPhone, Google backup on Android), your answers, results and settings may be included in them, like the data of your other apps. Those backups are managed by Apple or Google and tied to your account; we have no access to them. Uninstalling Civipass does not erase backups already made: you can manage them from your Apple or Google account. The purchase confirmation and the pending-purchase date are kept in a file left out of backups: on a new device, you get the full version back with the "Restore purchase" button.

Buying the full version

Payment is handled by Apple (App Store) or Google (Google Play), depending on your phone. They are responsible for it under their own privacy policies (Apple, Google). We never receive your name, your email address or your payment details.

To show the price, make the purchase, verify it and restore it, the app uses no intermediary and no third-party SDK: it talks directly to the App Store or Google Play billing service on your phone, which tells it whether your Apple or Google account owns the full version. The app receives that result on your phone, to unlock the purchased content or not; it is not sent to any Civipass server and we have no remote access to it. Those exchanges take place between your phone and Apple or Google, under their terms. The app only calls on that service in these cases:

  • when you open the full version's purchase screen: to show the price and check whether the purchase has already been made;
  • when you buy;
  • when you tap "Restore purchase";
  • if you bought, each time the app opens, to check that the purchase is still valid (that it has not been refunded, for instance);
  • if a purchase is awaiting confirmation, each time the app opens for seven days at most. If it is confirmed later, it will appear when you open the purchase screen again or tap "Restore purchase".

If you only use the free part without opening the purchase screen, the app does not call on that service.

Apple and Google provide us with monthly sales reports. Apple's are aggregated: for each product and country, they give the number of sales and the amounts, with no order number and no information about the buyer. Google's have one line per purchase: order number, date and time, product, amounts and taxes, device model, and the buyer's country, region and postcode. They contain neither your name, nor your email address, nor your payment details, but they are not anonymous: with the order number on your receipt, a line can be linked to your purchase.

For our accounting and tax obligations (Article 6(1)(c) GDPR), we only need the amounts. So we keep Apple's reports and, for Google, only the month's totals per app, transaction type and currency: we work them out as soon as the report arrives, then delete the detailed file we downloaded. The detailed reports remain available in the Google Play Console, where Google keeps them under its own rules; we only look at them there to keep our accounts or to answer a request from you.

Transfers outside the European Union

Civipass does not transfer your data outside the European Union. Apple and Google may process your data outside the Union, under their own policies, including Google for the mailbox where we receive your requests, under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (see "Handling your requests").

How long we keep your data

  • On your phone: until you uninstall the app or clear its data (backups already made are managed from your Apple or Google account). The pending-purchase date is erased after seven days, or as soon as the purchase is confirmed.
  • Sales reports: Apple's reports and Google's monthly totals, with no line per purchase, for six years from the last entry made in the accounting books (Article 30 of the Spanish Commercial Code). The detailed Google file we download is deleted as soon as the totals are worked out.
  • Requests about your data: while we handle them, then five years from their closing (see "Handling your requests").

What we do not do

  • No account, no ads, no analytics.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no selling or sharing of data for advertising.
  • No profiling or automated decisions.
  • The app does not ask for access to your contacts, photos, location, camera or microphone.

Children

Civipass is meant for adults preparing for the civic exam.

Your rights

You can ask for access to your data, its rectification, its erasure, the restriction of its processing or its portability, and, in the cases set out in the GDPR (Article 21), object to its processing. Write to civipassapp@gmail.com.

We do not know your name or your email address unless you write to us, and we keep nothing of what you do in the app. The only data about you that we have access to are the messages you have written to us and, if you bought on Google Play, the line for that purchase in the reports Google makes available to us in its console (we keep no copy of it). What the app stores on your phone is out of our reach, and Apple's reports contain no line per purchase. To find a line in a Google report, give us the order number shown on the receipt (it starts with "GPA."); without it, we will usually be unable to link it to you (Article 11 GDPR). If we have reasonable doubts that the request comes from you, we may ask for the additional information needed to clear them, and only that (Article 12(6) GDPR).

We reply within one month of receiving your request. If the request is complex or we receive many, this period can be extended by up to two months; we will then tell you so before the first month ends, with the reason and the new deadline (Article 12(3) GDPR).

Erasure. Uninstalling the app erases what it has stored on your phone, but not backups already made, which you manage from your Apple or Google account. Apple's reports and Google's monthly totals, which contain no line per purchase, are kept for as long as the law requires. Google's detailed reports remain in the Google Play Console, under Google's rules, and the purchase itself is recorded by Apple or Google in your account, under their own rules.

You can also complain to a data protection authority: the CNIL in France, or the AEPD in Spain, where the controller is based.

Handling your requests

When you exercise your rights, we keep your message and our reply (and therefore your email address), the right exercised, where relevant the last characters of the order number, the dates and the actions taken. We use them to receive your request, answer it and follow it up, which the GDPR requires of us (Article 6(1)(c), read with Articles 12 to 22 GDPR). After the request is closed, we keep them for five years, in a space with restricted access, only to be able to show that it was handled properly and to defend ourselves against a complaint: this retention is based on our legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). These five years are not set by the GDPR: they are a prudent period we chose, based on the general limitation period for personal claims in Spain (Article 1964(2) of the Spanish Civil Code) and in France (Article 2224 of the French Civil Code), which does not mean it applies to every claim. We keep only what that purpose needs: if a message contains attachments or information we do not need (an identity document, data about other people), we delete them. Every month, we delete what has reached five years.

We use Gmail to receive and keep these exchanges, on the basis of our legitimate interest in having a mailbox to receive and answer your requests (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). The data is therefore disclosed to Google Ireland Limited, which provides this service and does not act on our behalf: it processes the data as a separate controller, to provide and secure the mailbox and for the other purposes described in its terms and its privacy policy. Google may transfer them to Google LLC, in the United States, which is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework.

Security

The data stored on your phone is in the app's own space, which other apps cannot access; it is also protected by your phone's encryption and lock, depending on its settings. Exchanges with the Apple or Google purchase service are handled and secured by them.
